The three problems

parser · the shared parser read day-first dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. Their dates came out with day and month swapped: a booking for 7 April landed on 4 July. It reached us through the app, by email, and through the booking-v2 form. Engineering fixed it: fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales.

hint · booking-v3 asked for the month first. The booking-v3 form printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. The tenant’s people typed what the hint told them, and the form read the date day first. The swap looks the same as the parser’s, but the parser was not involved: the hint and the form disagreed. Only reports through the form of a booking-v3 tenant land here. Engineering will not fix it, because the booking-v3 form is retired.

one-day move · the date moves by one day, nothing is swapped. Tuesday shows as Monday. A client in Lisbon got Tuesday and a client in Toronto got Monday for the same meeting. An invite arrived before the booking was made. No day and month trade places, so the parser fix does not move these back, and they do not come from the booking-v3 form. It looks like a time-zone shift. The desk read these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json); the table above files them under parser by tenant and footnotes them there.

How to tell them apart

  • Not by subject. All three problems share the same subject lines.
  • Not by intake’s tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser; the table shows how often that guess disagrees with the tenant.

  • By reading the body. A one-day move names no swapped date. The desk notes these by id.
