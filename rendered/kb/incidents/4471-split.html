The fix queued for #4471 fixes the parser. The other two problems stay after it ships.

The three problems

parser · the shared parser reads day-first dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. Their dates come out with day and month swapped: a booking for 7 April lands on 4 July. It reaches us through the app, by email, and through the booking-v2 form. This is the bug #4471 was opened for.

hint · booking-v3 asks for the month first. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. The tenant’s people type what the hint tells them, and the form reads the date day first. The swap looks the same as the parser’s, but the parser is not involved: the hint and the form disagree. Only reports through the form of a booking-v3 tenant land here. Fix the hint (booking-v2 prints DD/MM/YYYY), not the parser.

one-day · the date moves by one day, nothing is swapped. Tuesday shows as Monday. A client in Lisbon got Tuesday and a client in Toronto got Monday for the same meeting. An invite arrived before the booking was made. No day and month trade places, so neither fix above moves these back. It looks like a time-zone shift. The desk read these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), and #4471’s table still counts them under parser, in a footnote.

How to tell them apart

  • Not by subject. All three problems share the same subject lines.
  • Not by intake’s tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser; the table counts where that guess names a different problem than the tenant does.
  • By tenant and channel. causes.ts knows each tenant’s locale and booking form: a day-first tenant’s report through a booking-v3 form is hint, every other one is parser.
  • By reading the body. A one-day move names no swapped date. The desk notes these by id.

What changes for the desk

  • #4471 keeps the parser reports. The hint and one-day reports belong to their own tickets.
  • When the parser fix ships, close the parser ticket only.
  • Subject-line merging hid two bugs in this ticket. Decision 0001 stays as accepted; a new decision record should replace it.
